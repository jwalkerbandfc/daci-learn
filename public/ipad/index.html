iPad guide

Outlook, Word and Copilot on iPad

Short, step-by-step help for everyday jobs. Pick a topic from the list, then follow the numbered steps in order.

Before you begin

  1. 1
    Check your iPad is connected to Wi-Fi.
  2. 2
    Install Outlook, Word and the Microsoft 365 Copilot app from the App Store if they are not already on your iPad.
  3. 3
    Sign in to each app with your work email address and password.

Buttons can look slightly different depending on your app version. If you cannot find something, look for the three dots … at the top of the screen. Most extra options live there.

iPad basics

Pinch to zoom

Make text and pictures bigger so they’re easier to read. This works in Word, Outlook, photos and web pages.

Two fingers on the screen

Steps

  1. 1
    Put two fingers on the screen, close together. Your thumb and first finger work well.
  2. 2
    Slowly spread them apart to zoom in. Everything gets bigger.
  3. 3
    While zoomed in, drag with one finger to move around the page.
  4. 4
    Pinch your fingers back together to zoom out.

Zooming only changes how big things look on your screen. It doesn’t change the text size in your document or how it prints. To make the text itself bigger, see .

Move through and select words

Before you can change, copy or colour text, you need to put the cursor in the right place or select the words. These taps do it quickly.

Taps at a glance

What each tap does
Touch and hold

Double-tap

The meeting is on Friday at 2pm. Please bring your notes.

Triple-tap

The meeting is on Friday at 2pm. Please bring your notes.

Press and hold to move the cursor

  1. 1
    Touch and hold your finger on the text. A blue line (the cursor) appears under your finger.
  2. 2
    Keep your finger on the screen and slide it left, right, up or down. The cursor follows your finger.
  3. 3
    Lift your finger when the cursor is where you want it. You can now type or delete from that spot.

Using the keyboard as a trackpad

  1. 1
    When the on-screen keyboard is showing, touch and hold the space bar.
  2. 2
    The letters go blank. Without lifting your finger, slide it around to move the cursor through the words.
  3. 3
    Lift your finger to place the cursor.

Select words

  1. 1
    Double-tap a word to highlight just that word.
  2. 2
    Triple-tap a word to select the whole sentence it’s in.
  3. 3
    To select more or less, drag the blue handles at each end of the highlight.
  4. 4
    A menu appears above your selection with options like Copy, Cut and Paste. Tap anywhere else to cancel the selection.

In some apps, triple-tap selects the whole paragraph instead of one sentence. If that happens, drag the handles to shorten it.

Outlook

To, Cc and Bcc

These three lines decide who gets your email and who can see who else got it.

What each line means

To
The main people. Use this for anyone who needs to read the email or do something.
Cc
“Carbon copy”. People who should know about it but don’t need to reply. Everyone can see who was copied in.
Bcc
“Blind carbon copy”. A hidden copy. Nobody else can see these names. Useful when emailing a large group so you don’t share everyone’s address.
What the new email screen looks like
To:Sam Lee⌄
Cc:Priya Shah
Bcc:
Subject: Team meeting moved to 2pm

Steps

  1. 1
    Open Outlook and tap New Email (the pencil icon).
  2. 2
    Tap the To line and start typing a name. Tap the right person when they appear in the list.
  3. 3
    To show the Cc and Bcc lines, tap the small arrow at the end of the To line.
  4. 4
    Tap Cc or Bcc and add names in the same way.
  5. 5
    Add a subject, write your message, then tap Send (the paper plane).

Added the wrong person? Tap their name, then tap the delete key on the keyboard to remove them.

Outlook

Flag an email

A flag marks an email so you remember to come back to it. Flagged emails show a small flag in your inbox.

From inside the email

  1. 1
    Tap the email to open it.
  2. 2
    Tap the three dots … at the top of the email.
  3. 3
    Tap Flag. To remove it later, follow the same steps and tap Unflag.

Faster: swipe in the inbox

  1. 1
    In your inbox, swipe left or right across an email.
  2. 2
    Tap Flag if it appears.
  3. 3
    If Flag doesn’t appear, set it up: tap your profile picture (top left), tap the gear icon, then Mail › Swipe Options, and choose Flag.

To see only your flagged emails, use the Flagged filter. The next page shows how.

Outlook

Filter your inbox

A filter hides everything except the emails you want to see, such as unread or flagged ones.

Filter options at the top of your inbox
FocusedOther
AllUnreadFlaggedAttachmentsMentions me

Steps

  1. 1
    Go to your Inbox.
  2. 2
    Tap the Filter button (a funnel shape) at the top of the email list.
  3. 3
    Choose what you want to see: Unread, Flagged, Attachments or Mentions me.
  4. 4
    To see all your emails again, tap Filter and choose All.

Focused and Other

Focused
Emails Outlook thinks are important to you.
Other
Newsletters, adverts and automatic emails. Check here if an email seems to be missing.

Word

Change text size

Make words bigger for headings or smaller to fit more on a page. Text size is measured in points; 11 or 12 is normal for body text.

The Home tab in Word
HomeInsertDrawLayout
Aptos ⌄11 ⌄BIUA ⌄

Steps

  1. 1
    Select the text. Double-tap a word, or triple-tap for a whole sentence, then drag the blue handles to cover all the text you want.
  2. 2
    Make sure the Home tab is open at the top of the screen.
  3. 3
    Tap the size box (the number, such as 11).
  4. 4
    Tap a new size from the list. The text changes straight away.

For headings, try the Styles button on the Home tab and pick Heading 1. All your headings will then match.

Word

Add colour to text

Colour can make key words stand out. You can change the colour of the letters or add a highlight behind them.

Change the letter colour

  1. 1
    Select the text you want to colour.
  2. 2
    On the Home tab, find the Font Colour button. It looks like the letter A with a coloured line under it.
  3. 3
    Tap the small arrow next to it and pick a colour.
  4. 4
    To go back to plain black text, choose Automatic.

Add a highlight

  1. 1
    Select the text.
  2. 2
    Tap the arrow next to the Highlight button (a highlighter pen) and choose a colour.
  3. 3
    To remove it, choose No Colour.

Stick to one or two colours. Dark text on a light background is the easiest for everyone to read.

Word

Change the font

The font is the style of the letters. Word usually starts with Aptos or Calibri.

Steps

  1. 1
    Select the text you want to change.
  2. 2
    On the Home tab, tap the font name box (for example Aptos).
  3. 3
    Scroll through the list and tap a font. Each name is shown in its own style so you can preview it.
  4. 4
    If a font has a cloud icon next to it, tap it once to download it first.

Bold, italic and underline

B
Bold makes letters thicker.
I
Italic slants the letters.
U
Underline draws a line under the text.

Use one font for the whole document. Change the size or make it bold for headings instead of mixing lots of fonts.

Printing · Outlook

Print an email

Your iPad prints using AirPrint. The printer must support AirPrint and be on the same Wi-Fi network as your iPad.

Steps

  1. 1
    Open the email you want to print.
  2. 2
    Tap the three dots … at the top of the email.
  3. 3
    Tap Print.
  4. 4
    Tap Printer and choose your printer from the list.
  5. 5
    Set the number of copies. Tap Options to choose double-sided or black and white, if your printer offers them.
  6. 6
    Tap Print at the top right.

Need to print an attachment instead? Tap the attachment to open it, tap the share icon or …, then tap Print.

Printing · Word

Print a Word document

The same AirPrint rules apply: a compatible printer on the same Wi-Fi as your iPad.

Steps

  1. 1
    Open the document in Word.
  2. 2
    Tap the File button (a page icon at the top left), or the three dots ….
  3. 3
    Tap Print. If Word asks, choose AirPrint.
  4. 4
    Choose your printer, the number of copies and which pages to print.
  5. 5
    Tap Print.

Printer not showing?

  1. 1
    Check the iPad and printer are on the same Wi-Fi network.
  2. 2
    Turn the printer off and on again, then wait a minute.
  3. 3
    Still stuck? Ask your IT team. Some work printers need extra setup.

Copilot

Get started with Copilot

Copilot is an AI assistant from Microsoft. You type or say a question, and it writes an answer. It can draft emails, summarise long text and suggest ideas.

Steps

  1. 1
    Open the Microsoft 365 Copilot app. If it’s not on your iPad, search for it in the App Store and install it.
  2. 2
    Sign in with your work email address.
  3. 3
    Tap Copilot or Chat to open the chat screen.
  4. 4
    Type your question in the box at the bottom, or tap the microphone to speak.
  5. 5
    Tap the send arrow and read the answer. You can reply to ask for changes, such as “make it shorter”.

Useful buttons

+
Add a file, such as a Word document, so Copilot can read it and answer questions about it.
Copy
Copy the answer so you can paste it into an email or document.
Work / Web
If you see this switch, Work lets Copilot use your work emails and files. Web uses public websites.

Copilot can get things wrong. Always read and check what it writes before you send or share it.

Copilot

Ask good questions

The clearer your request, the better the answer. Say what you want, who it’s for, and how long it should be.

A simple formula

  1. 1
    Goal: what do you want? “Write an email…”
  2. 2
    Context: why, and who is it for? “…to my team about the new rota…”
  3. 3
    Style: how should it sound and how long? “…friendly, under 100 words.”

Try these

“Write a short, friendly email to the team saying Friday’s meeting is moving to 2pm.”

Drafting an email

“Summarise this document in five bullet points for my manager.”

Add the file with + first

“Rewrite this paragraph so it’s easier to read.”

Paste your text after the request

“Give me three subject lines for an email about the staff training day.”

Getting ideas

Never type passwords, bank details or private information about other people into Copilot unless your organisation says it’s allowed.
